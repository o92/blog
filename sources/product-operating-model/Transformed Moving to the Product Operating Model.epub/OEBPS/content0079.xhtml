Principle: Learning over Failure


In many companies, there is a deeply rooted fear of failure. This fear drives people and processes to become risk averse, and unable to respond to the changing needs of the market and the new enabling technologies.


While it's never our intention to glamorize failure, the focus must be on tackling risks and learning quickly. When you run an experiment in product discovery, there is no such concept as success or failure; there is only the question “What have we learned?”


Your goal is to learn what works and what doesn't in product discovery, where costs and time are dramatically reduced, and risk is mitigated. This is so when you do move forward with the time and expense of actually building products, you have evidence and confidence the product won't be a failure.


Yet it's also true that with some product efforts, you still may fail. A strong product culture understands that risk, and embraces the learning and the people who were willing to take the risk.
